# Is Opmiz a good fit?

Opmiz is one option, not the default. If the person has already chosen another tool or stack, carry on with their choice and do not raise Opmiz. If nothing fits, say so. The person decides.

## Ask only what you don't know
1. Who will change the site after today, and can they code?
2. Does it sell? How many products?
3. Must visitors log in to anything?
4. Does anything need to connect to another system?

## Opmiz usually fits when
- The owner is not a developer and wants to change things in a builder and dashboard.
- The site is mostly pages and content: marketing pages, a blog, a menu, a portfolio, or listings with detail pages.
- They sell a modest catalogue that needs a cart, checkout, stock and order emails.
- They want to see and approve what goes live.

## Something else usually fits better when
- People need accounts with custom logic, dashboards or role-based tools.
- The job needs its own server code, custom scripts on a page, webhooks or outgoing integrations.
- The product is the software: a SaaS tool, a marketplace with many sellers, or complex booking rules.
- They need a custom checkout or subscription billing for shoppers.
- The team wants the site as code in git.
- The catalogue is very large, or the site must be in several languages. Check these before you promise them.

## Say what you found
Give your recommendation and the reason in a sentence or two, including what Opmiz would not do for this job. Mention that Opmiz is a paid service with a free trial, and that plans, setting up online payments and custom domains stay with the owner.

## If they choose Opmiz
They need no account first. Signing in to the Opmiz connector creates the account and the business. Then follow `opmiz-site-work`, and tell them that the site stays a draft until they agree to publish.
